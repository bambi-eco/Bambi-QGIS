name=Bambi - QGIS Integration
qgisMinimumVersion=3.22
qgisMaximumVersion=4.99
supportsQt6=True
description=Detect and track wildlife in drone videos/photos with geo-referencing
version=9.1.0
author=Christoph Praschl, Anna Maschek, David C.Schedl
email=christoph.praschl@fh-hagenberg.at

about=A comprehensive QGIS plugin for detecting, tracking, and geo-referencing wildlife in aerial drone recordings (videos or still photos) from DJI Enterprise drones with thermal and/or RGB cameras.
    Features include:
    - Frame extraction and undistortion from drone videos (SRT timestamps) or photo collections (EXIF)
    - Animal detection using YOLO models (auto-downloaded from HuggingFace)
    - Multi-object tracking with built-in, BoxMOT, or geo-referenced backends; import of pre-computed TRex tracklets
    - Demographic classification of tracked animals: cross-modal RGB/thermal track matching, DINOv3 features, occlusion/species/sex classifiers aggregated per animal by vote, and a size-based juvenile flag (requires a Hugging Face token for the gated DINOv3 model)
    - Segmentation tool: SAM3 / SAM 3.1 on the extracted frames by text or clicked point prompts, per frame or tracked across a sequence, through the Roboflow API, transformers, or Meta's sam3 package; masks geo-referenced, added as layers and exported as GeoJSON
    - Geo-referencing of detections, tracks, and SAM3 segmentations onto a DEM (real-world UTM coordinates)
    - Flight route visualization and perpendicular distance sampling for transect-based surveys
    - Survey analytics over several flights at once: census summary (counts, densities and sex/age breakdown per species and flight), track inventory, kernel-density heatmaps, coverage maps, a survey-wide orthomosaic, line-transect distance-sampling density/abundance estimation (from detections or tracks), and transect-based population estimation (naive, bootstrap, zero-inflated negative binomial)
    - Survey plot and exclusion zones drawn or imported in QGIS, limiting every survey analytic to the census area
    - Environmental context of a flight from public data, no account needed: Sentinel-2 indices (NDVI, vegetation moisture, open water, snow), ESA WorldCover land cover and tree cover, topographic wetness from the DEM and weather at flight time - as map layers, as attributes of every tracked animal and as habitat charts of the animals against the monitored ground
    - Per-frame camera field-of-view footprints and coverage areas
    - Georeferenced map products: per-frame GeoTIFFs, true orthomosaics, and ALFS light-field mosaics
    - DEM import: automatic download (Austria), GeoTIFF conversion, or flat surface meshes for aquatic surveys
    - Calibration wizards for camera intrinsics (single-camera SfM, stereo RGB+thermal) and per-flight pose corrections
    - 3D Flight Viewer: the flight in 3D (DEM, route, corrected camera poses and frustums) with a replay that drapes the frames, detections or tracks and live ALFS integrals onto the DEM, interactive correction factors with an automatic sharpness-based search, and a 3D reconstruction (sparse points and a dense DEM-seeded surface, RGB- or thermal-textured)
    - Companion tools: result video creator, radiometric thermal image viewer (DJI Thermal SDK), randomized transect flight planner, and interactive map-canvas inspectors
    - Key-frame based labelling tool: review detections/tracks on extracted frames (thermal or RGB), draw and edit track bounding boxes with species/sex/age/occlusion classes, interpolate between key frames, and propagate boxes of one or several tracks across frames via DEM geo-referencing
    - Transect splitting tool: split a flight into named transects (frame ranges) on the extracted frames, with a flight-route overview map, flight-path length measurement and an "end after X metres" helper
    - Results stored in GeoPackages that carry their own meaning: every detection has an id the later steps refer to, user-defined fields travel through the whole pipeline, and each step records what it produced and what it invalidated
    - Export to COCO, YOLO, MOT, TRex tracklets, GeoJSON (animals or segmentations), Camtrap DP and Darwin Core Archive (GBIF publishing)
    - Several flights per QGIS project, each with its own name, target folder, configuration and layer group (Thermal / RGB subgroups, fixed layer order), analysed together as one survey
    - Built-in dependency manager to install all required and optional packages from within QGIS
    - Animals readable at every zoom: bounding boxes with a colour per individual up close; zoomed out, pins in the colour of the species (colour-blind-safe defaults, configurable per species) carrying the animal's sex and age
    All results are automatically added as styled layers to the QGIS project, grouped per flight.

changelog=Version 9.1.0
    Added:
    - Environmental Context (Pre-Processing tab): publicly available data for the ground a flight monitored, on or near the day it flew, to help explain where animals were and how well a camera could see them. Four sub-steps, one per source, each of which can be run, re-run and fail on its own - a source that cannot be reached fails its own step only, and "Run All Four" carries on past a failure. E1 Satellite Indices: NDVI (greenness), NDMI (vegetation moisture - a proxy for how wet a patch is, not air humidity), NDWI (open water) and NDSI (snow) from the Sentinel-2 scene nearest to the flight, before or after, that is clear over the flight's own extent (judged from the scene classification there, not from the tile's cloud percentage); cloudy cells are left empty and the scene's date, its distance from the flight in days and its clear share are recorded. E2 Land Cover: ESA WorldCover 2021 classes at 10 m and the tree cover derived from them. E3 Terrain Wetness: the topographic wetness index computed from the flight's own DEM - static, no download, works under forest. E4 Weather: hourly Open-Meteo values at flight time (air and soil temperature, humidity, dew point, wind, cloud, radiation, snow depth) and over the 24 hours before (rain, sunshine, temperature range, hours since rain); model values for the flight as a whole. No account is needed for any of them; E1, E2 and E4 need an internet connection. The layers cover the flight's footprint plus a buffer, widened to the survey area when one is defined, on one 10 m grid in the flight's CRS, and are written to the flight's "environment" folder. "Add Environment Layers to QGIS" adds them as styled layers in a "BAMBI Environment" group below the camera groups, one visible at a time. Settings under Configuration > Environment (scene window, required clear share, buffer). See docs/environment.md.
    - Habitat Context (Survey Analytics tab): the environment where the animals were. "Summarise Habitat" writes one row per counted track (habitat_tracks.csv / .json) with the mean of every layer within a radius of the animal (30 m by default), its land-cover class, the flight's weather and the satellite scene's distance from the flight; one row per transect (habitat_transects.csv) with the means over its monitored area when the population estimate has run; and habitat.json. "Show Habitat Charts" compares, per layer, the distribution at the animals with the ground the cameras actually saw, and per land-cover class the share of the animals with the share of the ground (selection ratio with its standard error) - both sides measured over the same radius, so animals placed at random show no preference - with a species selector and a table of each flight's scene and weather. "Add Habitat Tracks to QGIS" loads the per-track table as a point layer. The population estimate itself is unchanged.
    - Animals on the map become pins when zoomed out. Zoomed in, an animal is its bounding box as before, and every tracked individual keeps a colour of its own for its box and path - now taken from the track id, so a track looks the same on its own layers and on the detection layers and keeps its colour when the layers are added again. Beyond a map scale set under Configuration > Project > Map Symbols (1:500 by default, can be switched off) the box becomes a teardrop pin of fixed screen size whose tip is its centre, in the colour of the species, with the sex as the mark in its middle (the female or male symbol, "?" when unknown) and the age as a small ringed badge on its lower right ("J", "A"; none when unknown). Each species has a default colour from the colour-blind-safe Okabe-Ito palette (red deer orange, roe deer blue, wild boar green, an animal of undetermined species black; the same in every flight) that can be changed in the Project Schema dialog (Species > Colour...), stored with the flight. A detection that belongs to a track takes its track's species, sex, age and box colour. It is an ordinary rule-based QGIS style, editable per layer in the Symbology dialog; "Apply to Layers in QGIS" restyles layers already added, and changing a colour in the Project Schema does so by itself. The detection and track layers gain the attributes species, sex, age, sex_mark, age_mark and box_colour. Layers added by an earlier version keep their look until they are added again.
    - Survey Report (Survey Analytics, "Write Survey Report" at the end of the tab): what the survey's tools found, in one HTML file that needs nothing else - no pictures beside it, no style sheet, no script, no connection - and that opens in any browser, can be sent as it is and prints (to a file, for a PDF). In it: the figures a reader looks for first; what was flown, when and in what weather; a map drawn into the page with the survey area, exclusion zones, the ground the cameras saw, the routes and the animals in the colours of their species, and optionally a picture of the map as QGIS shows it; the counts per species with groups, sex and age and the flights one by one; every density the survey has - census, grid, distance sampling, transects - in one table with its 95 % interval and what it rests on; clumping, hot spots and the habitat charts; the visits over time with the fitted rate of change; the reaction to the drone; how far off the places may be; the settings of the survey and what made each flight's data; and the method notes of every result. The report computes nothing: it reads the results that are there (survey_report.html lists each file with when it was written) and names what was not run. Only the census is built first where it is missing or was made for other flights or species. A result that was made when other flights or species were chosen is named at the top of the page and in the log, and one that cannot be read costs its own part of the page only. Where the flights were flown on several days the page says that the counts are those of all of them together, and leaves the census density over the survey area out. Written to analytics_{t|w}/survey_report.html in the survey folder and opened when it is done; "Open Report" opens the one written last. The title and whether the picture of the map goes in are saved with the QGIS project. See docs/pipeline.md, "Survey report".
    - Example flights (Input tab, "Download Example Flights..."): the flights of the public BAMBI dataset on Zenodo, to tick by their number. The list is the dataset's own: it is read from the summaries its repository (github.com/bambi-eco/Dataset) keeps for its downloader, and the files are fetched as its download_from_zenodo.py fetches them, so new parts and corrected labels arrive without a new plugin version. The list shows a flight's split and whether the dataset has its labelled version and its recordings; it can be put in order by any column and narrowed by typing; "Select all" ticks every flight that is shown, "Select none" unticks them. Every ticked flight goes into a folder of its own, gets its elevation model from the open worldwide models (GEM-15, see below) and is added to the project as a flight with its settings stored. "Download video data" (ticked) fetches a flight's video in the version chosen beside it, checked against the size and checksum Zenodo gives: "Processed" (the default) is the labelled dataset's undistorted, synchronised video with everything its archive holds - its frames are extracted at once as "Extract Frames" would, the left half of each as the thermal frame and the right half as the RGB frame, with the published poses and masks, so the flight is ready for the Processing tab; "Raw" is the recordings as they came off the drone, with SRT files, flight log and calibrations, which the steps from "Extract Frames" on work with. Unticked, only the poses and labels are fetched, a few seconds per flight, for a fast overview in QGIS, and the video can be fetched later. "Import flight route": the published poses become the flight's own, and the route is on the map before a frame is extracted. "Import labels": the animals labelled by hand on key frames become label tracks of the flight with the boxes between the key frames filled in; sex and age are read as the dataset documents them; a frame is found by the moment it was taken and a box scaled to the plugin's own picture, and after every extraction that changes the frames the labels are placed anew. "Use the published correction": the camera correction published for the flight is set, with the angle between the map's north and true north taken out of its rotation. The button stops the download while it runs; a flight that fails does not cost the others. docs/installation.md, "Example flights".
    - Elevation for anywhere: "Download DEM (worldwide)" (Input, Auto-Download) makes a flight's elevation model from an open model of the whole earth - no account needed. Two can be read, and the list beside the button chooses. The terrain model, Google's GEM-15 (2026, experimental): the bare ground at 15 m, 60 deg S to 84 deg N without Russia, China, Mongolia and Greenland. The surface model, the Copernicus DEM: 30 m, everywhere, with the canopy of a forest in it. "Best available", the default, takes the terrain model where it has the flight and the surface model where it has not. GEM-15 lies a few metres off, region by region, and has no values on water and snow: it is levelled to the Copernicus DEM on open ground (where its own surface and terrain models agree) and its gaps are filled from it, and the log says by how much. Against Austria's laser-scanned 1 m terrain model on eight areas the heights were off by 5.6 m (root mean square) with GEM-15 levelled and 9.1 m with the Copernicus DEM; under trees by 6-10 m against 11-19 m; in open fields by a metre or two with either. Only the part under the flight is transferred (a few seconds), with at least 150 m of ground around it; the flight's position is read from the AirData CSV or the SRT files, the mesh is made in the UTM zone the flight lies in (the target CRS is offered to be set to it), and the result - gem15_dem.glb, .json and .tif, or copernicus_dem.* - is set as the flight's DEM; its metadata says what it was made from. Where the Copernicus 30 m release has no tile the 90 m one is used; sea is ground at height nought. Neither is a laser scan: docs/dem.md has the comparison, and says what to set under Position Uncertainty.
    - Position Uncertainty (Survey Analytics): how far off may the place of an animal be? For every detection the survey counts an error budget is put together from how well the camera's position and height, its angle, the terrain model, the pixel and the timing are taken to be known (set by you; presets for satellite positioning and RTK, saved with the project) and from the geometry of that detection: the height of the camera above the animal, how far to the side it looked, the drone's speed, and the slope at the animal read from the DEM mesh - ground that falls away from the camera stretches every error. The result gives per detection the error along and across the line of sight and the two together, per animal the median of its boxes, and over all detections and per flight the median, what nine in ten stay within, the worst, and which part matters most. It opens as a table, is written as position_uncertainty_{source}.json, _boxes.csv and _tracks.csv, and "Add Uncertainty to QGIS" draws a circle around every animal within which its true place almost certainly lies.
    - Reaction to the Drone (Survey Analytics): did the animals flee? For every tracked animal of the ticked flights a constant velocity is fitted through all positions of its track, so that the jitter of the positions is told apart from movement: an animal moved when it went at least a set distance (3 m by default) while in view and three times the standard error of that distance - and, where a minimum speed is set beside the distance ("at ... m/s or faster"; any speed by default), at least that fast, so that an animal drifting through a long track does not count as having moved. Both thresholds can be set per species ("Per Species..."): a species without values of its own takes the general ones. What it covered is split into the part away from the line the drone flew and the part along it. Over all animals, per species and per flight: how many moved, how many of them away from the line and how many towards it (exact test), the mean speed away from the line with an interval, and whether the animals nearer the line were the faster - in a word: moved away from the line, moved in no particular direction, no sign of a reaction, or too little to tell. Movement along the line and all one way is named as what a terrain model that is off looks like. The result opens as a table, is written as drone_reaction.json and drone_reaction_tracks.csv, and "Add Movements to QGIS" draws an arrow per animal that moved, coloured by where it went.
    - Distance sampling: canopy cover and flight height can explain detectability (Survey Analytics, "Let canopy cover and flight height explain detectability"). Beside the two plain detection functions, models are fitted whose width depends on the tree cover around each animal (from the Land Cover step) and on the height the camera was above it (from the frame and the DEM), alone and together, and the model with the lowest AIC is used - a condition that explains nothing is left out. Each observation then stands for the animals its own strip width says it does, and the interval is measured between the lines plus what not knowing the coefficients adds. The result says what each condition does in the units of the survey ("the effective strip half-width is 38 m at 0 % canopy and 11 m at 80 %"), which conditions were not used and why; it needs a condition to be known for every observation and at least 20 observations. Off by default: without the tick the result is what it was.
    - Compare Visits (Survey Analytics): the same ground surveyed again, weeks or years apart. The ticked flights are divided into visits by the local day they were flown - or by a set number of days between two flights; flights less than six hours apart stay together, so a night survey does not end at midnight. Each visit is counted per species: animals, groups, sex and age, and the density over the ground its flights looked at, with a 95 % interval that takes a group of animals as one sighting, and with a survey area the number in it. Then what changed: an exact test of every visit against the one before and of the first against the last, and from two visits on a fitted rate of change - over the time the visits span and, from a year on, per year - with its interval and a plain verdict (increase, decrease, no clear trend). The comparison can be limited to the ground every visit saw. The result opens as a chart and a table per species, with the time of day and the air temperature of each visit, and is written as trend_{source}.json and trend_{source}_visits.csv; the two settings are saved with the project.
    - Review Queue (Survey Analytics, "Review Queue..." next to the track inventory report): the tracks worth a human look, for the flights and the camera of the survey, the most doubtful first and each with the reason - boxes of low confidence, very few boxes, no species, a species or sex a classifier called by a narrow vote, the detector's own classes saying another species than the track carries, the matched track on the other camera saying something else, no partner on the other camera although the cameras were matched, hidden on most frames. Tracks that were approved, labelled by hand or ruled out are not listed. Selecting a track shows it in the inspector; it is then approved (A), ruled out as not-an-animal (X), given another species, sex or age, or skipped (N). The limits for confidence, number of boxes and share of votes can be set in the window. The track inventory has two new columns: what the detector's classes called the animal (detector_species) and what a person decided on it (by_hand).
    - Accuracy report (Processing tab, "Compare with Hand Labels..."): on a flight that was gone through in the Labelling Tool, the pipeline's own output is compared with the labels. Were the animals found - precision and recall of the detector's boxes, per species, and what both would have been at every confidence, with the confidence at which their balance is best. Was each animal one track - animals in several pieces, tracks on nothing that was labelled, and what a census would have counted against what was there. Was it named right - confusion tables for species, sex and age, also per classifier model. A label of the species not-an-animal counts as a confirmed false alarm. The overlap two boxes need (0.3 by default) and the frames that were gone through can be set; the report is also written as accuracy.html, accuracy.json and accuracy_boxes.csv into the flight's folder.
    - Grid Density (Survey Analytics): the survey's ground - the survey area, or without one the ground the flights saw - is cut into equal cells and counted cell by cell. Cells are hexagons (the default), squares, triangles or rectangles with sides of their own; their size is given by area (1 ha by default) or by edge length. A cell's density is its animals over the ground the cameras saw in it, not over the size of the cell; cells of which less than a set share was seen (half by default) stay on the map as "too little seen" and are left out of the statistics. Ground flown more than once is read as the average over the flights or as the highest flight. Per cell: animals and density per species, sex and age, groups, the environment the cell lies in, and whether it is a hot or cold spot (Getis-Ord Gi*). Over the cells: the mean density with an interval from resampling the cells, the total for the survey area, the variance-to-mean ratio with its test, Moran's I, and the share of cells with animals. The grid is laid from the origin of the map's coordinate system, so the same ground is the same cell in every survey. "Add Grid to QGIS" loads the cells with two styles, by density and by hot spots; the results are grid_density_{source}.json, _cells.csv and _by_flight.csv.
    - Groups (Survey Analytics): the survey tools know which animals were together. Two animals of one species no further apart than the species' group distance - 50 m unless the Project Schema sets another one ("Group Distance...", next to the colour; 0 = never grouped) - belong to one group, and so does everything joined to them in the same way. They must have been seen together: tracked animals within 60 seconds of each other, boxes in the same frame, so an animal counted on two overlapping legs of a flight is not a group of two. The Census Summary reports the groups, their mean size and the largest per species and flight, lists every group (groups_{source}.csv / .json) and "Add Groups to QGIS" outlines the groups of two or more on the map; the Track Inventory and the inspector say which group an animal was in.
    Changed:
    - "Download sample implementations..." moved from the Detection tab to Configuration > Project (Custom Implementations): the samples cover every kind of custom code - detectors, tracker, classifiers, embedding model, importer and exporter - not detection alone.
    - Custom exporter: detections, tracks and segmentation masks can be exported in a format of your own. Choose the format "Custom exporter..." under Processing > Export (one flight) or Survey Analytics > Export (several flights with splits) and pick your .py file in the Custom exporter box. A class with setup(context) and run(data, output) is handed the flight's frames, detections, tracks and masks as plain Python - species as names, positions in pixels, in the project CRS and as longitude / latitude - and writes its files into the output folder. Sample: exporter_csv.py.
    - Dataset Export (Survey Analytics > Export): the ticked flights as one dataset in any export format, with train / validation / test splits made of whole flights - a flight and all its labels are in one split and never in two. Each flight is given its split in a table, or all are dealt at random by shares with a seed. Writes <folder>/train, /val, /test with a folder per flight, splits.json and dataset.json. For COCO and YOLO the classes are named alike over all flights (species ids are a flight's own); COCO gets one annotations.json per split, YOLO one data.yaml over all flights. A folder holding an earlier export in which a flight was in another split is refused. With "Only animals confirmed by the other camera" ticked in the Survey box, only the boxes of tracks the other camera saw too are exported. The export of one flight stays on the Processing tab.
    - Custom import: detections or whole tracks made by another tool can be imported with your own code, as the TRex import does for TRex (Configuration > Tracking > Custom import). A class with setup(context) and run(source, frames) returns one row per box - frame, box, confidence, class and, for tracks, a track id. The plugin stores the boxes under a source of their own, undistorts boxes given on the raw video, places them on the ground and records the tracks. A class named as a species of the project is that species. Sample: importer_csv.py.
    - Custom embedding model: step C1 can use a model of your own in place of DINOv3 (Configuration > Classification > Custom embedding model). A class with setup(context) and run(crops, items) returns one vector per crop; storage, resuming and the matched input work as with DINOv3, and the run is recorded under the file's name and hash. The published classifiers read DINOv3 vectors only and are refused on other embeddings - use classifiers trained on yours. Sample: embedder_colour_histogram.py.
    - Survey Analytics: "Only animals confirmed by the other camera" in the Survey box. Every tool then counts only the tracks of the survey's camera that Match Tracks paired with a track of the other camera (and, counting boxes, their boxes) - fewer false positives, at the price of leaving out what only one camera could see, so the numbers are a lower bound. The covered area is the ground both cameras looked at. A ticked flight with tracks and no matching run is refused by name. Results name the camera as "Thermal, confirmed by RGB". Off by default; saved with the project.
    - Survey Analytics: the tools are on four sub tabs instead of one long page - Overview (census, track inventory, survey report), Maps (density heatmap, grid density, coverage map, survey orthomosaic), Population & Behaviour (distance sampling, population estimation, compare visits, reaction to the drone, habitat context) and Quality (review queue, position uncertainty). The survey, the species and the survey area stay above them, for every tool. The Review Queue button moved from the track inventory to a section of its own on Quality. Nothing stored changes.
    - 3D Flight Viewer: a compass in the top right corner of the view says which way it looks. Its top is the view's direction, the red needle and the N are where the map's north is on the screen, and the bearing is written below ("NE 47"); it follows orbiting and "Follow camera" alike. "Show compass" on the View tab hides it.
    - 3D Flight Viewer: a scale bar in metres at the bottom of the view, taken from the elevation model's own coordinates. It holds where the middle of the view meets the terrain and moves on to the next round length (5 m, 10 m, 20 m, ...) as the view zooms. "Show scale" on the View tab hides it.
    - Review Queue: a track's number is a link, as the first and last frame are in the track inventory - underlined and blue - and a click on it opens the track in the inspector. It is the only thing that does: selecting a row no longer opens the inspector by itself, and the box "Show the selected track in the inspector" is gone.
    - Habitat Context: every chart of the result window says what its axes are of. A histogram names its quantity with the unit under the horizontal axis ("Tree cover (%)") and "Share" beside the vertical one; the land-cover bars have a scale under them ("Share of ground / of animals (%)") and a heading over the classes.
    - The dock's tabs are as tall as the tab in front. Every tab used to take the height of the longest one (the Classification configuration), so the shorter tabs - Pre-Processing, most configuration sub-tabs - were followed by a long stretch of empty space to scroll past before the buttons and the log below. Longer tabs scroll as before; the dock's width does not change between tabs.
    - For developers: the main panel's code is kept by tab. bambi_dock_widget.py was one class of 13,800 lines; it is now the frame around the tabs, and each tab's code is a module of its own in dock/ (dock/__init__.py says what is where). Steps are run by one step runner (dock/step_runner.py), which also makes it safe to start a step from the end of the one before. The panel is the same widget with the same controls and settings - nothing changes in how the plugin behaves.
    - Distance sampling takes a group of animals as one observation. The detection function is fitted to the groups, each at the distance of its middle from the line, and the density of animals is the density of groups times the mean group size, whose uncertainty is part of the confidence interval. Twelve deer in one meadow counted as twelve sightings claimed a precision the survey did not have. Where animals were together, n is therefore smaller and the interval wider than before; with animals on their own, or a group distance of 0, the result is unchanged. The result says how many animals the groups held and warns when the larger groups were the ones seen from further away.
    - What a person decides in the review queue stands. A species, sex or age set there is marked on the track as set by hand, and a later classifier run, a re-vote at another quorum and "Sync Labels Across the Match" leave it alone - on the track and on its boxes - also where the reviewer decided that it cannot be told. Until now only tracks drawn in the Labelling Tool were protected: a classifier run replaced whatever stood on a track the tracker had made.
    Fixed:
    - "Run All Four" (Environmental Context) no longer takes QGIS down when the second source starts.
    - The flight planner no longer deletes its target folder. A plan started by removing the whole folder, with everything else in it; it now replaces only the files of an earlier plan.
    - Population estimate (ZINB): the standard error and interval are those of the estimate. They were far too narrow: the transects' uncertainties were added up as if independent - as the reference R analysis does - while all fitted counts come from the same four parameters. In simulations the old interval held the true density about one time in four; the corrected one about nine in ten. The density itself is unchanged; its standard error and interval are wider than in reports made with earlier versions, and the interval is now log-normal and cannot reach below zero.
    - Distance sampling counts only the line that was surveyed. The whole flight log counted as effort - take-off, the transit to the first transect, the way home - which made the line too long and the density too low. The lines are now a flight's transect legs where transects are defined (animals seen in transit are left out and reported), otherwise the camera's path from the first to the last recorded frame; with a survey area, the part of each line inside it.
    - Distance sampling measures the encounter-rate variance between the lines (Buckland et al. 2001) wherever there are two or more - transect legs or several flights. The Poisson figure used so far assumes evenly spread animals and is too small for clumped ones; it remains only for a single line. The report says which was used and lists every line with its length and count.
    - What a survey counts - animals or boxes - is one setting for all its tools ("Count", in the Survey box), saved with the project. The census, the density heatmap and distance sampling each had a selector, with different defaults: the census counted animals, the other two boxes, so a density came out as boxes per hectare unless the selector was changed for each tool. All of them now count animals (tracks) unless the survey is set to boxes. The population estimate and habitat use, which only knew tracks, follow the setting too, so a still-photo survey without tracking can use every tool.
    - A tracked animal has one species in every survey tool: the track's own where it was identified, otherwise what most of its boxes were called by the detector. The tools disagreed about the same animals: the census, the track inventory and habitat use called every track "animal" that no classifier had named, whatever the detector's class mapping said (so a species filter found none of them); the density heatmap counted an animal once for each species its boxes had been called, at two places; distance sampling and the population estimate went by the last box.
    - A track that is not an animal is not counted by the census and habitat use. A track whose boxes the detector's class mapping calls "not-an-animal" was counted as an animal.
    - Distance sampling and the population estimate count each observation as what it is now. The species was the one written with the perpendicular distances, so an animal classified, relabelled or marked as a false positive afterwards was still counted as before until the distances were calculated again.
    - Population estimate (ZINB): with fewer than 30 transects the result warns that its interval is likely too narrow still, and points to the bootstrap.
    - Population estimate: a flight without animals is pooled with zero animals instead of being refused - whether nothing was detected on it, none of the selected species, or nothing inside the survey area. Leaving such flights out overstated the density. The check before the run no longer asks such a flight for perpendicular distances, which it cannot have.
    - Perpendicular distances of RGB detections and tracks are measured from the RGB camera's frames. Both steps took the thermal camera's positions whichever camera the boxes were from, so an RGB box was measured from an unrelated thermal frame.
    - Species, sex and age are voted on although some boxes have an occlusion label. As soon as any box of a flight had one - a single hand-labelled track is enough - every frame without a label counted as occluded, so the tracker's tracks had no frame left to vote with and got no species. Calls of the occlusion classifier left over from before the animals were detected again did the same. Occlusion is now decided frame by frame: the classifier's call, else the stored label, else the frame votes; the log says how many frames voted without a label.
    - "Fall back to RGB / thermal" works for animals only one camera saw. A classifier set to "Matched" reads both cameras side by side; the fallback handed it the one camera's vector, which is half as wide, so the classifier refused it and the whole classification run stopped. Such an animal is now called by the published classifier of that camera for the same task, and each frame's stored result names which classifier called it.
    - A flight added with "default configuration" starts as a new project does. It took the values that a project saved before a setting existed is loaded with: photo mode instead of video mode, crop padding 0.10 where the classifiers were trained with 0.25 (so their embeddings were cut differently from what they know), the first-frame orthomosaic instead of the average, and no measured flight-log margin. Adding a flight with "copy configuration" switched the form to photo mode as well. The stored default of the crop padding is 0.25 now, too.
    - Matching thermal and RGB tracks no longer gives up a right pair for two marginal ones. The pairing matched as many tracks as possible before it looked at any distance, so in a herd an animal seen 0.2 m apart by the two cameras could lose its partner to two pairs that just cleared the gate. A track may now stay unmatched at the price of half the gate, which takes a pair exactly when it is closer than the gate.
    - Age (size-based): animals are compared with their own species. All animals of a flight were one cohort, so among red deer an adult roe deer - half the size at any age - was called a juvenile. A species with too few animals to compare is not judged, animals of no known species are compared with each other, and a track that is not an animal is left out. One such track (or one "unknown") no longer stops every unidentified animal from being measured.
    - Age (size-based): twins are both juveniles. The rule asked for a wide gap directly above the animal, so of two fawns of a size only the larger one - the one next to the gap - was called, and the smaller one an adult.
    - The inspector opens the box that was clicked. It found the box by class and confidence, so of two boxes of one frame with the same class and (to three decimals) the same confidence both counted as clicked, and the track shown - and offered for deletion - was the first one's. A box whose species had been classified after the layer was added was not found at all, and its polygon stayed on the map when it was deleted. Boxes are now identified by their id.
    - The Roboflow API key no longer appears in the log. Roboflow takes the key in the address of the request, and every network error quotes that address, so a wrong key, a timeout or a dropped connection wrote the key into the log and the error message. Failures are now reported without it, and API keys and tokens are masked in the error text of every step.
    - The default models are the versions this release was tested with. Every default model - the detectors, the Re-ID weights, the classifiers, the DINOv3 backbone and SAM3 - was fetched from the current state of its repository, and a download counted as good when the file was larger than a megabyte: a model could change under a survey unnoticed, and a download cut off half-way was kept and used. They are now fetched at a fixed commit and checked against their published size and SHA-256, also when they are already on disk; a download is put in place only once it is complete. A file that is another version is replaced and kept as "*.previous"; without a network it is used, with a note in the log. Models and repositories of your own are not touched.
    - A step can finish while the Project Schema or the Class Mapping dialog is open. The dialogs kept their edits in an open database transaction until they were closed, which locks the project file: a step that finished meanwhile could not record its results ("database is locked"). Edits are now made on a private copy and written into the project when OK is pressed.
    - Cancelling the Project Schema dialog cancels everything. Renaming a custom field rewrote the stored values at once, before OK: Cancel took the name back but left the values under the new one, and the values of a field deleted "with its values" were gone although the field was still there. Stored values now change when OK is pressed, and only then.
    - Renaming or deleting a custom field leaves a field of the same name in another scope alone. A track field and a detection field may share a name; the values of both were renamed or discarded together.
    - Cancelling the camera calibration, closing a tool window or unloading the plugin while work is running no longer takes QGIS down. Work that cannot be interrupted (a 3D reconstruction, a model loading, a mesh being read) was waited for a few seconds and its thread then destroyed while still running, which ends the whole program. Such a thread is now left to finish by itself.
    - Downloading the elevation model for a flight that crosses the edge between two tiles no longer needs some 20 GB of memory and as much disk space. The tiles were merged whole and written out again before being cut to the flight; now only the flight's area is read from them. The resulting elevation model is the same, cell for cell.
    - The automatic time zone is right for night flights. It was found from the mean times of day of the recording and of the flight log: a video across midnight came out as UTC-10 and one after midnight as UTC-22 where UTC+2 was right, so the frames were matched to the wrong part of the log or to none of it. The date-times are now compared, which also gives offsets that are no whole hours (India, Nepal). The thermal SRT files are read as well - a thermal-only flight had no detection - the shown offset follows the files that are chosen, and the files are no longer read again for every query of the form.
    - Frames that are not square ("Preserve original aspect ratio") are handled as such by the inspector and the Correction Wizard. The boxes projected into the other camera, the cross hair of a map click and the Correction Wizard's rays and preview assumed square frames; on 16:9 frames everything sat too far from the image centre, sideways by the aspect ratio - a point near the frame's edge landed outside it - while the geo-referencing itself was right. Two boxes of the same class and confidence are also no longer projected as one box twice.
    - Stereo calibration saves the thermal camera's whole distortion. With a thermal calibration loaded, the points are placed on frames undistorted with it; the fit then started from "no distortion" and saved only what was left over, as if that were the camera's distortion - a file that undistorts raw thermal frames by a fraction of what they need. The points are now taken back to the raw frame and the loaded calibration is refined in full. A stereo calibration made on top of a loaded thermal calibration with an earlier version should be run again.
    - "Add Tracks to QGIS" no longer needs the legacy tracks.csv. It refused unless the tracks folder held a .csv file - written only while "Also write legacy text outputs" is on, and never for animals labelled by hand - although it read nothing from the file: the tracks come from the store. Every further .csv in that folder also added all tracks once more. Georeference Detections, Track Animals and Calculate Track Distances asked for the legacy folders in the same way; they now ask the store, like every other step.
    - A 5.x folder can still be migrated after its settings were saved. The flight's settings are kept in the folder's project.gpkg, which appears as soon as the folder is a flight's - when the QGIS project is saved, when a project from an earlier version is opened. That file counted as "this folder has been migrated": "Migrate 5.x..." disappeared and the migration refused to run, with nothing imported. A project.gpkg holding only settings no longer counts; one with added species or fields, or with results, still does.
    - A new QGIS project starts without flights. "New Project" emptied the form but left the previous project's flights in the selector, one of them still active: the target folder entered for the new project pointed that old flight at it, and saving the new project wrote the old project's flight list into it. The flights now go with their project, and the step statuses are cleared with them.
    - A folder's stored settings are loaded, not overwritten. A flight keeps its settings in its target folder, and a folder that has been processed before brings them along - but only for "Browse and Add..." and for a flight just added by name. The first flight of a project (target folder typed, browsed for, or filled in from the recordings' folder) and a flight pointed at another folder kept the form's values, and the next save - saving the QGIS project is one - replaced what the folder had stored: recordings, calibration, corrections, every threshold. All of these now ask first and load the folder's settings; what the form showed stays with the folder the flight leaves. A folder no flight has yet is not written to, a step does not start on settings that were just loaded or in a folder that was refused, and a loaded flight shows the folder it was read from rather than the path stored with it.
    - One survey, one folder. With no survey folder entered, the results go to "bambi_survey" next to the QGIS project - next to the first ticked flight while the project has not been saved. Saving the project for the first time therefore moved the folder: the census, the distances and the survey area written until then stayed behind, and the tools went on in a new, empty one. A default folder that holds results is now kept - it is entered in the field when saving the project, saving it elsewhere or ticking another flight first would move it. Empty the field to go to the new default.
    - TRex tracklets get their positions. The import projected the boxes itself and wrote the result only to georeferenced.txt and tracks.csv - files nothing reads since 6.0. Imported tracklets therefore had no position: "Add Tracks to QGIS" had nothing to add and the survey tools counted no animal. The import now records the boxes and tracks and lets Geo-Reference Detections place them, like any other box, with a reason recorded for each one that cannot be placed. Also: boxes made on the raw video are stored in the pixels of the extracted frames (they were stored in the video's, so crops and overlays sat beside the animal); the tracks are recorded beside a detector run or hand labels too (none was); the undistortion uses one focal length for frames of any shape, as the extraction does; and the text files follow "Also write legacy text outputs". Import the tracklets again to get the positions.
    - Exports say what is known about the animal. Species, sex and age are known per animal - on the track, where the classifiers, the labelling tool and the inventory put them - while its boxes keep what the detector called them, often just "animal". The exporters read the boxes alone: a classified and approved flight left as "animal" in COCO, YOLO, MOT, GeoJSON and Camtrap DP, and Darwin Core held every such animal back for having no species. A tracked box is now exported with its animal's species, and the animal's sex, age and approval travel with it (Darwin Core: sex, lifeStage, and identificationVerificationStatus "verified" once approved). A track marked not-an-animal takes its boxes out of the training formats. The MOT sidecar is written as proper CSV (a comma in a name shifted the columns).
    - The TRex export can be imported again. It wrote the arrays X, Y, conf and cls, while the TRex import reads detection_p, detection_class and pose points - importing an export stopped with an error. The export now writes the fields the import reads (the box as two pose points), numbers the classes per species instead of writing internal ids, lists them in classes.txt and names the frame size where it is known.
    - The MOT export is a MOTChallenge sequence. It wrote gt.txt beside the images with the detector's confidence in the "evaluate this row" column, classes counted from 0 (the benchmark tools know 1 to 13 and refuse 0), a tenth column and no seqinfo.ini, and copied only the frames with a box under their own names - so a tracker run on the images counted frames differently from the ground truth. Now: gt/gt.txt with the nine ground-truth columns (flag 1, class 1, visibility 1; a kept not-an-animal box is flag 0, class 8), seqinfo.ini (frame rate from the capture times, length, image size), every frame of the flight in img1 as 000001.jpg onwards, and the species, confidence, sex, age and approval in attributes.csv.
    - The Camtrap DP export follows Camtrap DP 1.0. The package named the profile and met little of it: no contributors, project, spatial, temporal or taxonomic in datapackage.json, no table schemas, no filePublic and fileMediatype on the media, no eventStart and eventEnd on the observations, and columns of its own that the schema does not know - readers that take the columns by position read it into the wrong ones. Now every table has the fields of its schema in the schema's order; a box is a media observation with its place on the frame, a tracked animal an event observation with species, sex and life stage; "human" or "machine" says who classified it; positions and custom fields are observationTags. A flight without any geo-referenced box is refused (its location was written as latitude 0, longitude 0), as are frames without a capture time and time zone. Add the people responsible to "contributors" before publishing.
    - Flight planner: four routing errors. (1) A plan in a CRS whose first axis is the northing - MGI / Austria Lambert and Gauss-Krueger, DHDN Gauss-Krueger, ETRS89-LAEA - was written about 200 km from the area: the coordinates were handed over easting first. UTM was right. (2) The Random strategy never returned where no route could reach the minimum number of transects, and wrote to log.txt until the disk was full; it now stops after "Number of retries" failed tries in a row. Random Loop counts every failed try and gives a discarded try's transects back to the plan. (3) Random Loop tested the way between a start point and a route against the no-fly zones from the wrong place (the start point's number was taken for its x), so start points behind a no-fly zone were accepted. (4) No-fly lines and points - a power line, a mast - were ignored; they now block what meets them. Also: every polygon of the area layer is planned for, not only the first. Plans for a given seed can differ from those of earlier versions.
    - A flight is never shown with another flight's settings. Switching to a flight whose folder was not there (a drive that is not connected) left the form as it was - the folder, recordings and every setting of the flight selected before; a project.gpkg that could not be read ended the switch with an error half way. Such a flight is now shown with the defaults and its own folder, the log says why, and nothing is written to it. The settings of a flight without a folder are kept in the QGIS project per flight: there was one place for all of them, read for any flight whose own store had nothing, so one flight could be shown - and then given - another's.
    - Class Mapping: OK no longer takes species away. Every box of the selected source was re-resolved from its raw class - also with nothing changed. A box a classifier had named, or that had its species synced across from the other camera, went back to what the mapping says (usually "animal"); and with the labelling tool's own source selected, which was listed like a detector and is the only one in a flight labelled by hand, every hand label became "animal". Now only the detector's own answers follow a changed mapping (boxes still saying what their class was mapped to before, or "animal"); hand labels are not listed and never touched; and the choices made for one detector are kept while another is looked at.
    - Custom fields with an umlaut, a quote or a backslash in their name. The stored values were searched for the name as typed, but are written as JSON - "Groesse" with its umlaut is stored with escapes. Such a field counted as unused (so it could be deleted without a warning), and renaming or deleting it left every value behind under the old name. The search now looks for the name the way it is stored.
    - Labelling tool: labels that could not be saved are not closed away. When saving failed on closing the tool, on loading another folder or on switching the camera - the project in use by a running step, a drive gone - the message "Could not save labels" was followed by exactly that: the window closed, or the other labels were loaded over the unsaved ones. The tool now stays as it is until the labels are saved or discarded on purpose; a failed autosave says so once and switches Autosave off.
    - "Add ... to QGIS" pressed twice shows the product once. A second press added a second group of the same name - tracks, frame detections, footprints, perpendicular distances, flight route, ALFS, GeoTIFFs - and left the first one standing empty, its layers gone with the files that were rewritten; a raster layer (orthomosaic, density, coverage) was simply there twice. The group or layer of that name is now replaced.
    - Density heatmap and coverage map: zero is a result, not "no data". Both rasters wrote their zeros as no data. Surveyed ground without animals looked like ground that was never surveyed, and a mean taken from the density raster - by the plugin or by zonal statistics - was the mean over the places where animals were; a gap in the coverage of a survey area was transparent like the world outside it, and not on the map at all where the area reached beyond the frames. Now ground known to be surveyed (the survey area, the cameras' footprints) carries density 0 where no animal was, and "mean_density" says what it is the mean over; inside a survey area a coverage of 0 is a value, shown in grey as "not seen", and the map reaches as far as the area. The density raster's cells are exactly the cells the animals were counted into (they were written slightly narrower). Generate both maps again to get this.
    - Track inventory: the "Distance to the flight line" column is filled. It was always empty: the distances file holds its entries under "tracks", and the inventory read the file itself as the list of entries. A distance is shown only when it was measured from one of the track's own boxes, so a file left from before the tracks were numbered anew does not put another animal's distance in the row.
    - Camera Calibration Wizard (stereo): four point pairs were enough to run it - eight equations for the nine numbers fitted for the thermal camera - and the result was shown in green with a tiny error whatever it was worth; with a pixel of imprecision in placing the points, points elsewhere in the frame were off by 15 to 30 px. At least 6 pairs are needed now and 12 or more recommended (the counter under the images says so); the calibration is checked on points left out of the fit, and the results page shows that error and turns amber, saying why, when there are few points, when they cover only part of the frame, when the fit does not hold for points left out of it, or when it arrived at numbers no lens has. A stereo calibration made from fewer than about 10 pairs is worth running again
    - Graphics memory: what a light-field render holds on the graphics card was not given back. Generate ALFS (per tile and per sample) and Export Frames as GeoTIFF (per frame) left each render's framebuffer behind - 24 bytes per pixel, about 400 MB for a 4096 px tile - until the step ended, and the sampling mode kept its whole render context when the step failed; the 3D Flight Viewer's ALFS tab kept a renderer for every image size it had seen (nearly one per frame over terrain) and for every focus height, its search for correction factors and a failed Correction Wizard preview kept a context each, for as long as QGIS ran. Every render now gives back all of it when it is done, however it ends
    - Generate ALFS: the mosaic spans the whole DEM, and a tile no frame reached had every frame of the flight read, uploaded and rendered into it before it was found empty and thrown away - for a flight over a corner of a large DEM that was most of the step's time (a test mosaic of 1024 tiles, 16 of them under the flight: 75 s, now 5 s). A tile gets the frames that can see it and is not rendered when there are none; the log says how many those were. How far a frame sees is taken from the matrices it is rendered with instead of an estimate that assumed a camera looking straight down: tilted by 20 degrees a frame lands 141 m away from 100 m up, the estimate stopped at 117 m, and the frame was missing from the tiles in between
    - 3D Flight Viewer, Reconstruction: every keyframe of the range was read before the reconstruction started and kept, as a float image, until it was done - 11 MB each at the default image size, 14 GB for the 5 km of a survey flight at the default keyframe spacing - plus 2 MB of feature descriptors per keyframe and, with occlusion, a mask of the whole surface grid per keyframe; a range of more than a few hundred keyframes ran out of memory. Keyframes are now read when a step needs them and at most about 1 GB of them kept, descriptors live only while a keyframe's neighbours are matched, the undistortion mask is shared, and what a camera sees is stored for the part of the grid it looks at. The results are the same
    - Download DEM (Austria): a tile was written straight to its place in the cache while it came in, and whatever was there afterwards was the tile - a download shorter than the server announced, an error page sent with "200 OK", the part left by closing QGIS - and was used by every later run ("Using cached"); a tile that failed with a network error was reported as "not found", after trying older editions in its place, and the elevation model built from the remaining tiles with a hole. A tile now comes in under <tile>.tif.part, is continued where a broken, cancelled or closed download stopped (when the server confirms it is the same file), tried again when the connection breaks, and gets its name only when it is as long as announced and a GeoTIFF whose contents all lie within the file; tiles already in the cache are checked the same way and fetched again when they are not whole; a tile that cannot be fetched stops the download with a message instead of being left out
    - Closing a tool: the tools tidied up in their close event only, which Escape and a tool's own Close or OK button do not raise - so the 3D Flight Viewer, the Thermal Viewer, the Feature Viewer, the AGL tool, the Flight Planner, the Video Creator and the Correction Wizard closed that way were hidden with their worker threads running, their files open and what they had loaded (the viewer's render context among it) still held, and the Camera Calibration Wizard kept its worker after it was accepted.     - Elevation mesh: a cell of an elevation model holds the height at its centre; the mesh put it at the cell's south-west corner, so the whole terrain lay half a cell too far to the south-west - 0.5 m with the Austrian one-metre model, 5 m with a ten-metre GeoTIFF (measured on a ten-metre model with a slope: every height 2 m above the ground at the place it was put).     - Labelling tool: "Add detections to project" keeps the track of every hand-labelled animal - the same track id, its "Approved" tick and its thermal/RGB pairing. Each export used to write them anew under new ids.
    - Labelling tool: a species whose name has capital letters ("Alpine ibex") is stored as that species. The name was lower-cased before it was looked up, so such labels were stored as "animal".
    - Labelling tool: merging label tracks that show a box on the same frames asks first. A merged track has one box per frame, so the merge took its key frames from the tracks in turn - two animals merged by mistake became one track jumping between them, with one animal's box gone on every shared frame, and nothing said so. The question names the tracks, the frames they share and how many boxes cannot be kept; it also comes up for merges proposed by "Find tracks to merge".
    - Frame-range corrections and the thermal curve are kept. Both were written to the QGIS project but read back from the flight's own store, so they were gone after reopening the project or switching flights, and the frames were then geo-referenced without the corrections. They are now stored with the flight like every other setting, and a new flight starts without the previous flight's frame ranges.
