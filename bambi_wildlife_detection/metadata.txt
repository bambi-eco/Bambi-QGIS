[general]
name=Bambi - QGIS Integration
qgisMinimumVersion=3.22
description=Detect and track wildlife in drone videos/photos with geo-referencing
version=9.1.0
author=Christoph Praschl, Anna Maschek, David C.Schedl
email=christoph.praschl@fh-hagenberg.at

about=A comprehensive QGIS plugin for detecting, tracking, and geo-referencing wildlife in aerial drone recordings (videos or still photos) from DJI Enterprise drones with thermal and/or RGB cameras.
    Features include:
    - Frame extraction and undistortion from drone videos (SRT timestamps) or photo collections (EXIF)
    - Animal detection using YOLO models (auto-downloaded from HuggingFace)
    - Multi-object tracking with built-in, BoxMOT, or geo-referenced backends; import of pre-computed TRex tracklets
    - Demographic classification of tracked animals: cross-modal RGB/thermal track matching, DINOv3 features, occlusion/species/sex classifiers aggregated per animal by vote, and a size-based juvenile flag (requires a Hugging Face token for the gated DINOv3 model)
    - Segmentation tool: SAM3 / SAM 3.1 on the extracted frames by text or clicked point prompts, per frame or tracked across a sequence, through the Roboflow API, transformers, or Meta's sam3 package; masks geo-referenced, added as layers and exported as GeoJSON
    - Geo-referencing of detections, tracks, and SAM3 segmentations onto a DEM (real-world UTM coordinates)
    - Flight route visualization and perpendicular distance sampling for transect-based surveys
    - Survey analytics over several flights at once: census summary (counts, densities and sex/age breakdown per species and flight), track inventory, kernel-density heatmaps, coverage maps, a survey-wide orthomosaic, line-transect distance-sampling density/abundance estimation (from detections or tracks), and transect-based population estimation (naive, bootstrap, zero-inflated negative binomial)
    - Survey plot and exclusion zones drawn or imported in QGIS, limiting every survey analytic to the census area
    - Environmental context of a flight from public data, no account needed: Sentinel-2 indices (NDVI, vegetation moisture, open water, snow), ESA WorldCover land cover and tree cover, topographic wetness from the DEM and weather at flight time - as map layers, as attributes of every tracked animal and as habitat charts of the animals against the monitored ground
    - Per-frame camera field-of-view footprints and coverage areas
    - Georeferenced map products: per-frame GeoTIFFs, true orthomosaics, and ALFS light-field mosaics
    - DEM import: automatic download (Austria), GeoTIFF conversion, or flat surface meshes for aquatic surveys
    - Calibration wizards for camera intrinsics (single-camera SfM, stereo RGB+thermal) and per-flight pose corrections
    - 3D Flight Viewer: the flight in 3D (DEM, route, corrected camera poses and frustums) with a replay that drapes the frames, detections or tracks and live ALFS integrals onto the DEM, interactive correction factors with an automatic sharpness-based search, and a 3D reconstruction (sparse points and a dense DEM-seeded surface, RGB- or thermal-textured)
    - Companion tools: result video creator, radiometric thermal image viewer (DJI Thermal SDK), randomized transect flight planner, and interactive map-canvas inspectors
    - Key-frame based labelling tool: review detections/tracks on extracted frames (thermal or RGB), draw and edit track bounding boxes with species/sex/age/occlusion classes, interpolate between key frames, and propagate boxes of one or several tracks across frames via DEM geo-referencing
    - Transect splitting tool: split a flight into named transects (frame ranges) on the extracted frames, with a flight-route overview map, flight-path length measurement and an "end after X metres" helper
    - Results stored in GeoPackages that carry their own meaning: every detection has an id the later steps refer to, user-defined fields travel through the whole pipeline, and each step records what it produced and what it invalidated
    - Export to COCO, YOLO, MOT, TRex tracklets, GeoJSON (animals or segmentations), Camtrap DP and Darwin Core Archive (GBIF publishing)
    - Several flights per QGIS project, each with its own name, target folder, configuration and layer group (Thermal / RGB subgroups, fixed layer order), analysed together as one survey
    - Built-in dependency manager to install all required and optional packages from within QGIS
    - Animals readable at every zoom: bounding boxes with a colour per individual up close; zoomed out, pins in the colour of the species (colour-blind-safe defaults, configurable per species) carrying the animal's sex and age
    All results are automatically added as styled layers to the QGIS project, grouped per flight.

changelog=Version 9.1.0
    Added:
    - Environmental Context (Pre-Processing tab): publicly available data for the ground a flight monitored, on or near the day it flew, to help explain where animals were and how well a camera could see them. Four sub-steps, one per source, each of which can be run, re-run and fail on its own - a source that cannot be reached fails its own step only, and "Run All Four" carries on past a failure. E1 Satellite Indices: NDVI (greenness), NDMI (vegetation moisture - a proxy for how wet a patch is, not air humidity), NDWI (open water) and NDSI (snow) from the Sentinel-2 scene nearest to the flight, before or after, that is clear over the flight's own extent (judged from the scene classification there, not from the tile's cloud percentage); cloudy cells are left empty and the scene's date, its distance from the flight in days and its clear share are recorded. E2 Land Cover: ESA WorldCover 2021 classes at 10 m and the tree cover derived from them. E3 Terrain Wetness: the topographic wetness index computed from the flight's own DEM - static, no download, works under forest. E4 Weather: hourly Open-Meteo values at flight time (air and soil temperature, humidity, dew point, wind, cloud, radiation, snow depth) and over the 24 hours before (rain, sunshine, temperature range, hours since rain); model values for the flight as a whole. No account is needed for any of them; E1, E2 and E4 need an internet connection. The layers cover the flight's footprint plus a buffer, widened to the survey area when one is defined, on one 10 m grid in the flight's CRS, and are written to the flight's "environment" folder. "Add Environment Layers to QGIS" adds them as styled layers in a "BAMBI Environment" group below the camera groups, one visible at a time. Settings under Configuration > Environment (scene window, required clear share, buffer). See docs/environment.md.
    - Habitat Context (Survey Analytics tab): the environment where the animals were. "Summarise Habitat" writes one row per counted track (habitat_tracks.csv / .json) with the mean of every layer within a radius of the animal (30 m by default), its land-cover class, the flight's weather and the satellite scene's distance from the flight; one row per transect (habitat_transects.csv) with the means over its monitored area when the population estimate has run; and habitat.json. "Show Habitat Charts" compares, per layer, the distribution at the animals with the ground the cameras actually saw, and per land-cover class the share of the animals with the share of the ground (selection ratio with its standard error) - both sides measured over the same radius, so animals placed at random show no preference - with a species selector and a table of each flight's scene and weather. "Add Habitat Tracks to QGIS" loads the per-track table as a point layer. The population estimate itself is unchanged.
    - Animals on the map become pins when zoomed out. Zoomed in, an animal is its bounding box as before, and every tracked individual keeps a colour of its own for its box and path - now taken from the track id, so a track looks the same on its own layers and on the detection layers and keeps its colour when the layers are added again. Beyond a map scale set under Configuration > Project > Map Symbols (1:500 by default, can be switched off) the box becomes a teardrop pin of fixed screen size whose tip is its centre, in the colour of the species, with the sex as the mark in its middle (the female or male symbol, "?" when unknown) and the age as a small ringed badge on its lower right ("J", "A"; none when unknown). Each species has a default colour from the colour-blind-safe Okabe-Ito palette (red deer orange, roe deer blue, wild boar green, an animal of undetermined species black; the same in every flight) that can be changed in the Project Schema dialog (Species > Colour...), stored with the flight. A detection that belongs to a track takes its track's species, sex, age and box colour. It is an ordinary rule-based QGIS style, editable per layer in the Symbology dialog; "Apply to Layers in QGIS" restyles layers already added, and changing a colour in the Project Schema does so by itself. The detection and track layers gain the attributes species, sex, age, sex_mark, age_mark and box_colour. Layers added by an earlier version keep their look until they are added again.
    Changed:
    - The dock's tabs are as tall as the tab in front. Every tab used to take the height of the longest one (the Classification configuration), so the shorter tabs - Pre-Processing, most configuration sub-tabs - were followed by a long stretch of empty space to scroll past before the buttons and the log below. Longer tabs scroll as before; the dock's width does not change between tabs.
    Fixed:
    - "Run All Four" (Environmental Context) no longer takes QGIS down when the second source starts.
    - The flight planner no longer deletes its target folder. A plan started by removing the whole folder, with everything else in it; it now replaces only the files of an earlier plan.
    - Population estimate (ZINB): the standard error and interval are those of the estimate. They were far too narrow: the transects' uncertainties were added up as if independent - as the reference R analysis does - while all fitted counts come from the same four parameters. In simulations the old interval held the true density about one time in four; the corrected one about nine in ten. The density itself is unchanged; its standard error and interval are wider than in reports made with earlier versions, and the interval is now log-normal and cannot reach below zero.
    - Distance sampling counts only the line that was surveyed. The whole flight log counted as effort - take-off, the transit to the first transect, the way home - which made the line too long and the density too low. The lines are now a flight's transect legs where transects are defined (animals seen in transit are left out and reported), otherwise the camera's path from the first to the last recorded frame; with a survey area, the part of each line inside it.
    - Distance sampling measures the encounter-rate variance between the lines (Buckland et al. 2001) wherever there are two or more - transect legs or several flights. The Poisson figure used so far assumes evenly spread animals and is too small for clumped ones; it remains only for a single line. The report says which was used and lists every line with its length and count.
    - What a survey counts - animals or boxes - is one setting for all its tools ("Count", in the Survey box), saved with the project. The census, the density heatmap and distance sampling each had a selector, with different defaults: the census counted animals, the other two boxes, so a density came out as boxes per hectare unless the selector was changed for each tool. All of them now count animals (tracks) unless the survey is set to boxes. The population estimate and habitat use, which only knew tracks, follow the setting too, so a still-photo survey without tracking can use every tool.
    - A tracked animal has one species in every survey tool: the track's own where it was identified, otherwise what most of its boxes were called by the detector. The tools disagreed about the same animals: the census, the track inventory and habitat use called every track "animal" that no classifier had named, whatever the detector's class mapping said (so a species filter found none of them); the density heatmap counted an animal once for each species its boxes had been called, at two places; distance sampling and the population estimate went by the last box.
    - A track that is not an animal is not counted by the census and habitat use. A track whose boxes the detector's class mapping calls "not-an-animal" was counted as an animal.
    - Distance sampling and the population estimate count each observation as what it is now. The species was the one written with the perpendicular distances, so an animal classified, relabelled or marked as a false positive afterwards was still counted as before until the distances were calculated again.
    - Population estimate (ZINB): with fewer than 30 transects the result warns that its interval is likely too narrow still, and points to the bootstrap.
    - Population estimate: a flight without animals is pooled with zero animals instead of being refused - whether nothing was detected on it, none of the selected species, or nothing inside the survey area. Leaving such flights out overstated the density. The check before the run no longer asks such a flight for perpendicular distances, which it cannot have.
    - Perpendicular distances of RGB detections and tracks are measured from the RGB camera's frames. Both steps took the thermal camera's positions whichever camera the boxes were from, so an RGB box was measured from an unrelated thermal frame.
    - Species, sex and age are voted on although some boxes have an occlusion label. As soon as any box of a flight had one - a single hand-labelled track is enough - every frame without a label counted as occluded, so the tracker's tracks had no frame left to vote with and got no species. Calls of the occlusion classifier left over from before the animals were detected again did the same. Occlusion is now decided frame by frame: the classifier's call, else the stored label, else the frame votes; the log says how many frames voted without a label.
    - "Fall back to RGB / thermal" works for animals only one camera saw. A classifier set to "Matched" reads both cameras side by side; the fallback handed it the one camera's vector, which is half as wide, so the classifier refused it and the whole classification run stopped. Such an animal is now called by the published classifier of that camera for the same task, and each frame's stored result names which classifier called it.
    - A flight added with "default configuration" starts as a new project does. It took the values that a project saved before a setting existed is loaded with: photo mode instead of video mode, crop padding 0.10 where the classifiers were trained with 0.25 (so their embeddings were cut differently from what they know), the first-frame orthomosaic instead of the average, and no measured flight-log margin. Adding a flight with "copy configuration" switched the form to photo mode as well. The stored default of the crop padding is 0.25 now, too.
    - Matching thermal and RGB tracks no longer gives up a right pair for two marginal ones. The pairing matched as many tracks as possible before it looked at any distance, so in a herd an animal seen 0.2 m apart by the two cameras could lose its partner to two pairs that just cleared the gate. A track may now stay unmatched at the price of half the gate, which takes a pair exactly when it is closer than the gate.
    - Age (size-based): animals are compared with their own species. All animals of a flight were one cohort, so among red deer an adult roe deer - half the size at any age - was called a juvenile. A species with too few animals to compare is not judged, animals of no known species are compared with each other, and a track that is not an animal is left out. One such track (or one "unknown") no longer stops every unidentified animal from being measured.
    - Age (size-based): twins are both juveniles. The rule asked for a wide gap directly above the animal, so of two fawns of a size only the larger one - the one next to the gap - was called, and the smaller one an adult.
    - The inspector opens the box that was clicked. It found the box by class and confidence, so of two boxes of one frame with the same class and (to three decimals) the same confidence both counted as clicked, and the track shown - and offered for deletion - was the first one's. A box whose species had been classified after the layer was added was not found at all, and its polygon stayed on the map when it was deleted. Boxes are now identified by their id.
    - The Roboflow API key no longer appears in the log. Roboflow takes the key in the address of the request, and every network error quotes that address, so a wrong key, a timeout or a dropped connection wrote the key into the log and the error message. Failures are now reported without it, and API keys and tokens are masked in the error text of every step.
    - The default models are the versions this release was tested with. Every default model - the detectors, the Re-ID weights, the classifiers, the DINOv3 backbone and SAM3 - was fetched from the current state of its repository, and a download counted as good when the file was larger than a megabyte: a model could change under a survey unnoticed, and a download cut off half-way was kept and used. They are now fetched at a fixed commit and checked against their published size and SHA-256, also when they are already on disk; a download is put in place only once it is complete. A file that is another version is replaced and kept as "*.previous"; without a network it is used, with a note in the log. Models and repositories of your own are not touched.
    - A step can finish while the Project Schema or the Class Mapping dialog is open. The dialogs kept their edits in an open database transaction until they were closed, which locks the project file: a step that finished meanwhile could not record its results ("database is locked"). Edits are now made on a private copy and written into the project when OK is pressed.
    - Cancelling the Project Schema dialog cancels everything. Renaming a custom field rewrote the stored values at once, before OK: Cancel took the name back but left the values under the new one, and the values of a field deleted "with its values" were gone although the field was still there. Stored values now change when OK is pressed, and only then.
    - Renaming or deleting a custom field leaves a field of the same name in another scope alone. A track field and a detection field may share a name; the values of both were renamed or discarded together.
    - Cancelling the camera calibration, closing a tool window or unloading the plugin while work is running no longer takes QGIS down. Work that cannot be interrupted (a 3D reconstruction, a model loading, a mesh being read) was waited for a few seconds and its thread then destroyed while still running, which ends the whole program. Such a thread is now left to finish by itself.
    - Downloading the elevation model for a flight that crosses the edge between two tiles no longer needs some 20 GB of memory and as much disk space. The tiles were merged whole and written out again before being cut to the flight; now only the flight's area is read from them. The resulting elevation model is the same, cell for cell.
    - The automatic time zone is right for night flights. It was found from the mean times of day of the recording and of the flight log: a video across midnight came out as UTC-10 and one after midnight as UTC-22 where UTC+2 was right, so the frames were matched to the wrong part of the log or to none of it. The date-times are now compared, which also gives offsets that are no whole hours (India, Nepal). The thermal SRT files are read as well - a thermal-only flight had no detection - the shown offset follows the files that are chosen, and the files are no longer read again for every query of the form.
    - Frames that are not square ("Preserve original aspect ratio") are handled as such by the inspector and the Correction Wizard. The boxes projected into the other camera, the cross hair of a map click and the Correction Wizard's rays and preview assumed square frames; on 16:9 frames everything sat too far from the image centre, sideways by the aspect ratio - a point near the frame's edge landed outside it - while the geo-referencing itself was right. Two boxes of the same class and confidence are also no longer projected as one box twice.
    - Stereo calibration saves the thermal camera's whole distortion. With a thermal calibration loaded, the points are placed on frames undistorted with it; the fit then started from "no distortion" and saved only what was left over, as if that were the camera's distortion - a file that undistorts raw thermal frames by a fraction of what they need. The points are now taken back to the raw frame and the loaded calibration is refined in full. A stereo calibration made on top of a loaded thermal calibration with an earlier version should be run again.
    - "Add Tracks to QGIS" no longer needs the legacy tracks.csv. It refused unless the tracks folder held a .csv file - written only while "Also write legacy text outputs" is on, and never for animals labelled by hand - although it read nothing from the file: the tracks come from the store. Every further .csv in that folder also added all tracks once more. Georeference Detections, Track Animals and Calculate Track Distances asked for the legacy folders in the same way; they now ask the store, like every other step.
    - A 5.x folder can still be migrated after its settings were saved. The flight's settings are kept in the folder's project.gpkg, which appears as soon as the folder is a flight's - when the QGIS project is saved, when a project from an earlier version is opened. That file counted as "this folder has been migrated": "Migrate 5.x..." disappeared and the migration refused to run, with nothing imported. A project.gpkg holding only settings no longer counts; one with added species or fields, or with results, still does.
    - A new QGIS project starts without flights. "New Project" emptied the form but left the previous project's flights in the selector, one of them still active: the target folder entered for the new project pointed that old flight at it, and saving the new project wrote the old project's flight list into it. The flights now go with their project, and the step statuses are cleared with them.
    - A folder's stored settings are loaded, not overwritten. A flight keeps its settings in its target folder, and a folder that has been processed before brings them along - but only for "Browse and Add..." and for a flight just added by name. The first flight of a project (target folder typed, browsed for, or filled in from the recordings' folder) and a flight pointed at another folder kept the form's values, and the next save - saving the QGIS project is one - replaced what the folder had stored: recordings, calibration, corrections, every threshold. All of these now ask first and load the folder's settings; what the form showed stays with the folder the flight leaves. A folder no flight has yet is not written to, a step does not start on settings that were just loaded or in a folder that was refused, and a loaded flight shows the folder it was read from rather than the path stored with it.
    - One survey, one folder. With no survey folder entered, the results go to "bambi_survey" next to the QGIS project - next to the first ticked flight while the project has not been saved. Saving the project for the first time therefore moved the folder: the census, the distances and the survey area written until then stayed behind, and the tools went on in a new, empty one. A default folder that holds results is now kept - it is entered in the field when saving the project, saving it elsewhere or ticking another flight first would move it. Empty the field to go to the new default.
    - TRex tracklets get their positions. The import projected the boxes itself and wrote the result only to georeferenced.txt and tracks.csv - files nothing reads since 6.0. Imported tracklets therefore had no position: "Add Tracks to QGIS" had nothing to add and the survey tools counted no animal. The import now records the boxes and tracks and lets Geo-Reference Detections place them, like any other box, with a reason recorded for each one that cannot be placed. Also: boxes made on the raw video are stored in the pixels of the extracted frames (they were stored in the video's, so crops and overlays sat beside the animal); the tracks are recorded beside a detector run or hand labels too (none was); the undistortion uses one focal length for frames of any shape, as the extraction does; and the text files follow "Also write legacy text outputs". Import the tracklets again to get the positions.
    - Embeddings: a box added to a frame later (a hand label, say) no longer costs the frame's other boxes their vectors. Only the new box is computed, and its vector replaced the frame's file; the others were then missing although listed as done, the next run computed them again and dropped the new one in turn. Vectors are now added to the frame's file, which is replaced in one step so an interrupted write cannot leave it truncated.
    - The built-in tracker's "Tracker Mode" and "Max Center Distance" work. The tracker matched by overlap with the Hungarian assignment whatever mode was selected and never read the centre distance, so GREEDY, CENTER and HUNGARIAN_CENTER did nothing. All four modes now do what they say (HUNGARIAN, the default, tracks exactly as before), the form enables the threshold the chosen mode uses, and the centre distance defaults to 2 m (it was 0.2 m, at which the centre modes would join next to nothing).
    - "Abort" stops tracking. It was not passed on to the trackers: the run went on to the end, was written, and was then reported as cancelled. Tracking now stops between two frames, before anything is written, so the previous tracks stay.
    - Boxes the tracker fills in between two detections are no longer recorded as detections. Each track got a member with the id -1, which refers to nothing and was reported as a missing detection.
    - Flights cannot be switched, added, removed or pointed at another folder while a step is running. The check looked at something that was never set, so it never held, and the log and status of the running step then described a flight no longer shown.
    - "Reset Step" works for the RGB camera. It always reset the thermal camera's step, whichever camera was selected, so RGB results could not be reset at all. The list now offers every step that has results, per camera.
    - Running a classifier again replaces its earlier answers. The species of an animal the new run calls differently stayed on its boxes - where the exports and the analytics read it - because anything but "animal" counted as someone else's answer; and a sex or age the new run no longer calls (the vote fell below the quorum) stayed on the track. What a classifier wrote is now on record, so a later run replaces it or takes it back, and leaves alone what the detector's class mapping, the label sync or a person put there.
    - Results kept after a reset no longer attach to the new boxes and tracks. "Reset Step" (or deleting the file) keeps what depended on the step and marks it out of date; but the new boxes started numbering at 1 again, so the kept geo-referencing, tracks, matches and classifications belonged to them - new boxes at the old map positions, in the old tracks. Boxes and tracks now number on from every number still referred to in the flight's folder.
    - "Out of date" is shown on every step it applies to. The panel showed it for classification and track matching only; detection, geo-referencing, tracking and the rest stayed "Completed" whatever had run again before them. Flight route, ALFS, GeoTIFFs, orthomosaic, the perpendicular distances, segmentation and BoxMOT tracking were never on record as having run, and so could not go out of date at all.
    - A label keeps replacing the tracker's track of its animal after the tracker has run again, and a label merged from two tracker tracks replaces both. The replacement went by the track's number alone, which changes with every tracker run.
    - A hand-labelled animal is one track. Label tracks are written as tracks when they are added to the project, but the trackers took their boxes as input as well, so every labelled animal was tracked a second time and counted and exported twice. With nothing but labelled boxes, "Track Animals" now finishes without an error and without tracker tracks.
    - Detecting again with another model replaces the previous boxes. Only the boxes of the model running now were removed, so those of the model that ran before stayed, and every animal was there twice - in the tracks, the counts and the exports. The same happened when detecting again on a flight whose detections had been migrated or adopted from detections.txt.
    - Extracting the frames of a video again replaces the images. An image that already existed was kept, so with another calibration or "preserve aspect ratio" the new poses and mask were written around the old pictures; and with another sampling rate or range the frames of the earlier extraction stayed in the folder beside the new ones.
    - Labelling tool: the labels are opened from the project's store, which is what the rest of the plugin reads and changes. The tool used to read its own labels.json alone, so a labelled track deleted in the inspector came back with the next save, a species renamed in the Project Schema lost its labels, and a missing or damaged labels.json made the next save empty the stored labels. labels.json is still written, and is loaded instead when it is the newer of the two (for example copied in from another computer); where the two disagree, the side that is not loaded is kept as a file.
    - Labelling tool: a track corrected through "Import as label track" stays the only one counted. Every save in the tool dropped the note that the label replaces the tracker's track, so the animal counted twice until the next "Add detections to project". Removing the labels from the project now also ends the replacement.
    - Labelling tool: "Add detections to project" keeps the track of every hand-labelled animal - the same track id, its "Approved" tick and its thermal/RGB pairing. Each export used to write them anew under new ids.
    - Labelling tool: a species whose name has capital letters ("Alpine ibex") is stored as that species. The name was lower-cased before it was looked up, so such labels were stored as "animal".
    - Labelling tool: merging label tracks that show a box on the same frames asks first. A merged track has one box per frame, so the merge took its key frames from the tracks in turn - two animals merged by mistake became one track jumping between them, with one animal's box gone on every shared frame, and nothing said so. The question names the tracks, the frames they share and how many boxes cannot be kept; it also comes up for merges proposed by "Find tracks to merge".
    - Frame-range corrections and the thermal curve are kept. Both were written to the QGIS project but read back from the flight's own store, so they were gone after reopening the project or switching flights, and the frames were then geo-referenced without the corrections. They are now stored with the flight like every other setting, and a new flight starts without the previous flight's frame ranges.
